A UTM is the small piece of text on the end of a link that tells your analytics where a visitor came from and why they clicked. This guide explains what UTMs are, how Consequential uses them, and the handful of rules that keep them useful.

## What is a UTM?

A **UTM** (Urchin Tracking Module) is a set of parameters added to the end of a URL. They do not change the page the visitor lands on. They tell your analytics system which platform, campaign, and ad sent the visitor.

In simple terms:

- UTMs tell your analytics tool **how someone found your site**
- They are used on **ads, emails, and social media links** — anywhere you control the link
- Without UTMs, most traffic looks generic and cannot be tied back to a campaign

Example:

```text
https://example.com/signup?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=spring_launch
```

This link tells you:

- The visitor came from **Google** (`utm_source`)
- It was a **paid ad** (`utm_medium=cpc`)
- It belonged to the **Spring Launch** campaign (`utm_campaign`)

## Why it matters

UTMs help you answer questions like:

- Which ad platform brings the best customers?
- Which campaign drives the most orders, not just the most clicks?
- Which email or social post actually converted?
- Where should the next dollar of budget go?

In Consequential, the [Acquisition Dashboard](/acquisition-dashboard/understand-the-acquisition-dashboard/) classifies every order into a channel and, where it can, a campaign. It works from the acquisition signals on the visit that led to the order: the UTMs on the landing URL, the referring site, and the store's own referrer data. Of those, the UTMs are the only signal you control. On an ad, an email, a social post, or a partner link, they are what turns "a visit from facebook.com" into "a visit from *this* campaign". A link with no UTMs falls back to whatever the referrer says — often just **Direct** or **Unattributed** — and a link with inconsistent UTMs is split across several rows that should have been one.

<Aside type="caution">
A missing `utm_source` or `utm_medium` is the most common cause of `(not set)` and unattributed traffic. See [Why you see (not set) in your Google Analytics reports](/academy/attribution/not-set-in-google-analytics/).
</Aside>

## The standard UTM parameters

| Parameter | What it records | Example values | Required |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| `utm_source` | The platform or site the visitor came from | `google`, `facebook`, `klaviyo`, `tiktok`, `newsletter` | Yes |
| `utm_medium` | The type of traffic | `cpc`, `paid_social`, `email`, `organic_social`, `affiliate`, `referral` | Yes |
| `utm_campaign` | The campaign, promotion, or product push | `spring_launch`, `bfcm_2026`, `new_arrivals` | Yes |
| `utm_term` | The keyword or audience that triggered the ad | `running_shoes`, `lookalike_1pct` | Optional |
| `utm_content` | The specific ad, link, or creative, to tell variants apart | `video_a`, `hero_banner`, `footer_link` | Optional |

`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` together are enough to attribute a visit. `utm_term` and `utm_content` add detail for comparing keywords, audiences, and creatives within a campaign.

## Best practices for UTM parameters

**Use lowercase everywhere.** `Facebook`, `facebook`, and `FACEBOOK` are three different sources to an analytics tool. Pick lowercase and never deviate.

**Fix a vocabulary for `utm_source` and `utm_medium`.** Decide once what each platform and traffic type is called, write it down, and reuse it. A short table is enough:

| Where the link goes | `utm_source` | `utm_medium` |
|---------------------|--------------|--------------|
| Google Ads | `google` | `cpc` |
| Meta ads | `facebook` | `paid_social` |
| TikTok ads | `tiktok` | `paid_social` |
| Email campaigns | `klaviyo` (or your ESP) | `email` |
| Organic social posts | `instagram`, `facebook`, `tiktok` | `organic_social` |
| Influencers and partners | the partner's name | `influencer` or `affiliate` |

**No spaces.** Spaces become `%20` in the URL and break copy-pasted links. Use underscores or hyphens: `spring_launch`, not `Spring Launch`.

**Make `utm_campaign` follow your naming convention.** If your campaigns already use the [ad naming convention](/faq/advertising-best-practices/ad-naming-conventions/), reuse the same name in the UTM with the spaces removed, so the platform, the dashboard, and the URL all agree on what a campaign is called.

**Do not put UTMs on internal links.** A UTM on a link from your own homepage to your own product page starts a new attributed session and overwrites the real source. UTMs belong only on links that bring people *to* your site from somewhere else.

**Do not put personal data in UTMs.** UTMs are visible in the URL, in browser history, and in any tool that receives the page URL. Never include an email address, a name, or a customer ID.

**Use a builder, not a keyboard.** A shared spreadsheet or a link builder removes typos and keeps everyone on the same vocabulary. Keep the sheet as the record of every tagged link.

**Let Google Ads tag itself.** Google Ads auto-tagging (`gclid`) plus a Final URL suffix with `ValueTrack` parameters gives you consistent UTMs without hand-typing them on every ad. See [Google Ads UTM and tracking setup](/integrations-platform-setup/ads-marketing-integrations/google-ads-utm-tracking-setup/).

## Final checklist before launching a campaign

- Every external link in the campaign has `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`
- All values are lowercase, with no spaces
- `utm_source` and `utm_medium` use the agreed vocabulary, not a new spelling
- `utm_campaign` matches the campaign name on the platform, spaces removed
- No UTMs on links between pages of your own site
- No personal data in any parameter
- The tagged link was opened in a browser and landed on the right page
- The link is recorded in the shared UTM sheet

## Summary

UTMs are five short parameters that turn "someone visited" into "someone visited from *this* ad in *this* campaign". Three of them — source, medium, campaign — are required for a visit to be attributed. The rules that matter are consistency rules: lowercase, no spaces, one vocabulary, one naming convention. Get those right and the Acquisition Dashboard can tell you what every channel is actually worth.
