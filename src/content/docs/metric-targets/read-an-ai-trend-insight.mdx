When a [trend alert](/metric-targets/set-a-trend-alert/) fires, Consequential opens an **AI trend** insight — a card on your Insights page that shows what moved, how far outside its expected range it landed, and what else moved with it.

<Aside type="note">
AI trend insights are rolling out alongside trend alerts. If the Insights page or the **AI trend** cards aren't in your workspace yet, they'll appear once the feature is enabled for you.
</Aside>

## Open an insight

<Steps>

1. Open **Insights** from the sidebar.
2. Set the date range in the header to the days you want to review. The feed shows only cards with an anomaly on one of those days, so a narrower range hides older moves.
3. Find a card tagged **AI trend**. The tags next to it show the metric, the store, and the period.
4. Click the card to open its detail page.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
If the feed reads *"No insights in this date range"*, widen the range. Nothing was flagged on the days you picked.
</Aside>

## The header

The top of the page tells you what kind of move this was:

- **Anomaly · Declining / Rising / Flat** — the direction the metric moved.
- **AI summary** — present when Consequential generated a written explanation, which appears under the title.
- **Live** — the metric is still outside its expected range. Without this badge, the move has resolved.
- The date it was detected, and the pillar it belongs to.

When several metrics moved together, the heading reads **AI trend · N metrics moved together**.

## The three numbers

| Tile | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **The metric on the detection date** | The actual value, with what percentage it is of the value the model expected. |
| **Gap against expectation** | How far off the expectation it landed, as an absolute gap and a percentage. Where available, it also shows the move as a multiple of the day-to-day variation the metric normally shows — a `3.0×` move is far more unusual than a `1.2×` one. |
| **Days outside range** | How many days in the recent window fell outside the expected range, such as *3 of the last 14*, plus what counts as normal for that day. More than one day means a pattern rather than a blip. |

## The tabs

The detail view has three tabs, each a different cut of the same move.

### Timeline

**Timeline** charts the metric around the detection date: the actual value, the same metric a year earlier for comparison, and the expected range as a band. Each fired breach is marked on its own date. When the card is a segment anomaly — Sales Channel = Hydrogen, for example — the chart shows that segment's own line, last-year line, and band, which is what it was judged against.

<Aside type="note">
The band is drawn only on days Consequential has a stored expectation for, and is left open where it doesn't — the gaps are honest, not missing data. A breach marker's own range can differ slightly from the band, because the check that fired it and the stored band come from separate model fits.
</Aside>

### Drivers

**Drivers** ranks the segments behind the move under a **Where this comes from** card. Each row is a dimension value — a sales channel, a country, a campaign — with a bar and a **Delta** that shows how much it added to or subtracted from the metric. Rank the list by one of three measures from the dropdown:

| Rank by | What it surfaces |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact** | Share of the whole metric's expected total — how much this segment alone moved the overall number. |
| **Lift** | Share of its own dimension's movement — most responsible for that dimension's spread, whatever the dollar size. |
| **Volume** | Plain year-over-year change, with no target involved. |

A row with a `›` arrow opens to the causes and movers found inside it, and those open further down the same way. A row that doesn't open is where the breakdown stopped.

### Breakdown

**Breakdown** is the full root-cause analysis: the path from the whole metric down to the segments that drove the move. It opens with a written summary — how much of the change the segments that broke their own range account for, and whether the move was concentrated in a few segments or spread across many.

Following the summary, each split (**Split by Sales Channel**, **Split by Country**, and so on) is a table of segments showing the **Actual** value, the **Expected (range)**, its **Share of change**, and a plain-language **Status**:

- **Below its range** / **Above its range** — a cause: the segment moved outside its own expected range and carries a real share of the change.
- **Moved most, within its range** — a mover: the segment stayed inside its range but swings enough day to day to drive the total anyway.
- **Moved the other way, made up for part of it** — the segment pushed against the change and offset some of it.
- **Within its range** — the segment moved in line with its usual size.

A segment marked with a `›` opens to its own split, so you can follow the cause down through the levels.

<Aside type="note">
When a detection has no stored analysis yet, the Breakdown tab says so. It fills in automatically once anomaly detection sends one — there's nothing to configure.
</Aside>

## What moved with it

The panel to the left of the tabs lists every detection on the card, each with a small trend line, switchable between:

- **By Dimension** — segments that moved together, for a metric with a segment-level breakdown.
- **By Metric** — other metrics that moved together, which is what makes a multi-metric card worth reading as one story rather than several.

Selecting an entry re-points the numbers, the chart, and the tabs at it, so you can compare members without leaving the page.
