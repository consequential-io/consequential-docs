Consequential scores every ad, campaign, ad set, and creative in **Analyze → Acquisitions** so you can see what is working at a glance. Each row gets:

- An **overall health score** from 0 to 100.
- A **letter grade** (A, B, C, or D) derived from that score.
- A **performance segment**: Winners, BAU (business as usual), or Underperformers.
- Six **health pillars** that explain *why* a row scored the way it did.

On the **Scores & Grading** settings page you control how all three are calculated. Changes apply to your tenant only and take effect the next time you load an Acquisitions view—no recompute or redeploy.

## Goal

Adjust the grade thresholds, performance-segment cutoffs, and health-pillar weights to match how your team judges ad performance.

## Prerequisites

- An Owner or Admin role on the tenant. Other roles see the settings page in read-only mode.
- Acquisition data in your account. Tenants without ad data have nothing to grade.

> **Note**: The default values reproduce Consequential's standard grading. If you never change these settings, your grades and segments stay exactly as they are out of the box.

## Configure grading

<Steps>

1. Open Consequential and go to **Settings → Scores & Grading**.

2. Set the **Letter grades** cutoffs.

   Each value is a point on the 0–100 overall health score. A row earns the grade whose threshold it meets or exceeds:

   | Grade | Earned when the health score is | Default |
   |-------|---------------------------------|---------|
   | A     | At or above the A threshold     | 75      |
   | B     | At or above the B threshold     | 50      |
   | C     | At or above the C threshold     | 25      |
   | D     | Below the C threshold           | —       |

   The thresholds must decrease in order: the A threshold must be greater than B, and B must be greater than C. **Save** stays disabled until the values are valid.

3. Set the **Performance segments** cutoffs.

   Segments rank each row against its peers by overall health score:

   | Segment        | Definition                        | Default |
   |----------------|-----------------------------------|---------|
   | Winners        | Top *N*% of rows                  | Top 20% |
   | BAU            | Everything between the two cutoffs | 60%     |
   | Underperformers | Bottom *N*% of rows              | Bottom 20% |

   Set **Winners (top %)** and **Underperformers (bottom %)**. BAU is the remainder, so the two values together cannot exceed 100.

4. Set the **Health weightage**.

   The overall health score is a weighted blend of six pillars. Enter a weight from 0 to 100 for each:

   | Pillar  | What it measures                          | Default weight |
   |---------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
   | Signals | Strength of the conversion and event signal | 15           |
   | Reach   | Audience reach and impressions            | 10             |
   | Click   | Click-through performance                 | 15             |
   | Convert | Conversion rate, ROAS, and cost efficiency | 30            |
   | Hook    | Early video retention (video creatives)   | 15             |
   | Watch   | Sustained video retention (video creatives) | 15           |

   Weights are normalized by their sum, so only the proportions matter—`30/20/10/10/10/10` and `3/2/1/1/1/1` produce the same score. At least one weight must be greater than 0.

   > **Note**: Hook and Watch apply only to video creatives. Consequential scores ad sets on the four non-video pillars—Signals, Reach, Click, and Convert.

5. Click **Save**.

</Steps>

## Verify your changes

1. Go to **Analyze → Acquisitions**.
2. Reload the view.
3. Confirm the grades, segment counts, and grade distribution reflect your new settings. For example, raising the A threshold from 75 to 85 moves some rows out of grade A on the next load.

## Reset to defaults

To return to Consequential's standard grading, set each field back to its default value from the earlier tables and click **Save**.
