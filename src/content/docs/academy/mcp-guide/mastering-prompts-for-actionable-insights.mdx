Well-written prompts play a critical role in helping Out of the Blue (OTB) deliver accurate insights, clear recommendations, and meaningful summaries, especially during the Insights and Recommendations step.

Clear, targeted prompts ensure that the agent understands exactly what to analyze and how to present the results, enabling faster decision-making and more actionable outcomes.

One proven way to structure effective prompts is the SMART framework, which stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. This framework helps you ask the right questions and get the most value from your data.

## Why prompt quality matters

In the Insights and Recommendations phase, your prompt acts as a set of instructions for the OTB agent. A well-crafted prompt helps to:

- Generate actionable insights. Clear instructions result in precise, relevant recommendations.
- Save time. Focus on key business questions without repeated iterations.
- Improve accuracy. Detailed prompts reduce ambiguity and align insights with your goals.

## Ensuring the right data context

### Stick to the data you provide

Even the most effective prompt depends on the data available in the Get Data step.

Out of the Blue agents can only analyze the datasets that have been explicitly provided. If your prompt references data that wasn't included, the resulting insights may be incomplete or inaccurate.

Best practice: always ensure your prompt only asks questions based on the data sources connected and selected during the Get Data step.

## The SMART framework for prompts

Use the SMART framework to structure prompts that lead to clear, actionable insights.

### Specific

Define what you want the agent to analyze or evaluate.

Instead of "Tell me about sales trends," try "Analyze our Q4 2024 online sales data to identify trends in customer behavior across different regions."

### Measurable

Specify the exact metrics or KPIs you want to review.

Instead of "What's going on with our website performance?", try "Evaluate website performance by comparing conversion rate, bounce rate, and average session duration between the first and last month of Q4 2024."

### Achievable

Ensure the request is realistic based on the data available in Out of the Blue.

Instead of "Predict our revenue for the next decade," try "Using historical monthly revenue data, forecast revenue trends for the next three months."

### Relevant

Focus on insights that directly support your business objectives.

Instead of "What should we change in our marketing?", try "Based on last quarter's marketing spend and customer acquisition data, recommend strategies to reduce cost per acquisition."

### Time-bound

Define a clear time range for analysis or recommendations.

Instead of "Give me an overview of performance," try "Summarize performance metrics from the past six months, focusing on customer retention and engagement trends."

## Customizing your prompts

While SMART provides a strong foundation, you can further improve results by tailoring prompts to your specific use case.

### Provide context

Include any background information that may influence the analysis.

Example: "Considering the recent website redesign, analyze its impact on user navigation and engagement over the past two months."

### Ask follow-up questions

Guide the agent to dig deeper into the insights.

Example: "After analyzing Q4 sales data, identify the key factors contributing to regional performance differences, including customer demographics and purchase behavior."

### Use comparative analysis

Comparisons help uncover trends and performance changes over time.

Example: "Compare website performance metrics between Q3 and Q4 and highlight the key differences impacting conversion rates."

## Practical examples

### Example 1: Improving conversion rates

Prompt: "Using our Q4 online sales data, analyze conversion rates across different traffic sources (social, email, and paid ads). Identify top-performing channels and recommend ways to optimize underperforming ones. Include metrics such as click-through rate and average order value for the last three months."

Outcome: Out of the Blue breaks down performance by channel, evaluates key metrics, and delivers targeted recommendations to help improve overall conversion rates.

### Example 2: Increasing customer retention

Prompt: "Review customer engagement data from the past six months and identify repeat purchase trends. Measure the average time between purchases and the percentage of returning customers. Based on the findings, recommend actions to improve retention, such as loyalty programs or targeted email campaigns."

Outcome: The agent focuses on measurable retention metrics and provides clear, actionable strategies to strengthen customer loyalty.

## Final thoughts

Writing effective prompts for the Insights and Recommendations step in Out of the Blue is both a skill and a process.

By applying the SMART framework, and enhancing it with context, comparisons, and follow-up questions, you unlock clearer insights, stronger recommendations, and better decision-making.

Experiment with different prompt structures, review the outcomes, and refine your approach over time. This continuous improvement helps you get the most value from Out of the Blue's AI-powered insights and confidently drive business growth.
